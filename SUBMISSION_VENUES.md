# Submission venues for the Habeas Protocol paper
*Researched 2026-05-01.*

## At-a-glance recommendation

Post the working paper to **SSRN (Legal Scholarship Network)** and **arXiv cs.CY** on day one — both are free, fast (24–48h), and establish a citable timestamp without burning a journal submission. Then split the paper into two streams in parallel: (a) submit the full long-form to **SCRIPTed** (free, peer-reviewed, fits law-tech 30k-word range) or **Stanford Journal of Blockchain Law & Policy** (free, fits the digital-asset / SICC angle), and (b) submit a tightened CS-flavoured version to **JURIX 2026** (Toulouse, December — submissions open ~Sep 2026) or **CS&Law 2027** as the primary peer-reviewed venue. Use **ProLaLa @ POPL 2027** for the Catala / formal-methods slice as a low-effort 3-page extended abstract. Practitioner blogs (Kluwer Arbitration, Oxford Business Law) take 1500-word adaptations and are the right vehicle to surface the empirical findings in front of arbitration practitioners — do these *after* the preprint is up.

## Notes on dual-submission etiquette

Posting to **SSRN** and **arXiv** is universally accepted alongside subsequent journal submission — in law, almost every journal expects an SSRN preprint before publication, and Springer/Elsevier/OUP/Wiley all explicitly permit "non-commercial preprint server" deposits. Diamond-OA journals (SCRIPTed, Internet Policy Review, EJLT, Lex Electronica, JBLP) all allow preprints. **Do not** dual-submit to two peer-reviewed *journals* simultaneously — this is a hard breach of etiquette in both law and CS. **Conferences are stricter**: ICAIL, JURIX, CS&Law, FAccT, AIES require the work to not be under simultaneous review at another peer-reviewed venue, though preprints are fine. **Blogs (Kluwer Arbitration, OBLB, Tech Policy Press) require exclusivity for the *blog post itself*** — they expect the post to be original to them, but you can freely adapt material from your preprint or accepted paper. The clean play: SSRN+arXiv day one → one journal submission at a time → blog adaptations after preprint goes up.
