Habeas Protocol

A constitutional reframing of the “Legal Operating System for Digital Worlds” thesis, grounded in empirical analysis of three operating special-jurisdiction tribunals: the DIFC Courts in Dubai, the ADGM Courts in Abu Dhabi, and the Singapore International Commercial Court (SICC). Updated April 28, 2026.

121 judgments coded against six per-ruling primitives a digital tribunal must satisfy, plus two architectural system properties. 39 form a hand-coded gold set (32 DIFC + 7 ADGM); the remaining 82 entries (69 ADGM + 13 SICC) are AI-triaged and AI-graded against the same rubric, with per-entry provenance. All three tribunals score at near-ceiling: SICC averages 1.95 / 2.00, ADGM 1.91, DIFC 1.72. The saturation pattern survives the 10× ADGM sample expansion (1.93 at n=7 → 1.91 at n=76) and replicates across a third operating tribunal in a different legal family (English-law-via-statute vs Singapore common law). All three score 2/2 on both system properties. Four executable traces from the corpus demonstrate the protocol covers static rules, deferred conditionals, rule-bounded human judgment, and arithmetic composition over substantive findings. The tribunals already exist; what is missing is the computational layer.

Tribunal comparison: mean primitive scores

Mean score per primitive, with DIFC and ADGM side-by-side. ADGM scores at or near ceiling on every per-ruling primitive (1.96 average across six). DIFC averages 1.73, weakest on the Enforcement Bridge primitive — where ADGM wins because ADGM judgments explicitly bind to UAE federal law via Cabinet Resolution.

Finding: Two operating tribunals, both implementing the full protocol at near-ceiling, available to plug in today. The Maxim thesis does not require building a tribunal from scratch; it requires layering computation on top of the ones that already exist.

Rules cited across the corpus

Frequency of cited instruments and clauses across the 39 judgments. RDC Part 38 (costs) is the single most-cited block, reflecting the corpus's heavy weighting toward costs and enforcement orders.

Three working traces

Each trace lifts a rule from the corpus into Catala source plus a Python predicate evaluator and runs it against an event log of the case facts. The three traces span the full spectrum of what the protocol can encode — and, in the case of trace #3, what it cannot.

Methodology note: the third trace is the methodologically necessary one — it shows what the rules cannot fully decide. The protocol bounds, but does not eliminate, the human-judgment residue.
