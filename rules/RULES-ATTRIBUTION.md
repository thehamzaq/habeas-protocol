# Source-text attribution for the rule library

Each `*.catala_en` file in `rules/` reproduces verbatim text from one or
more underlying legal sources (statutes, judgments, rules of court,
practice directions). This file consolidates, per source, the
copyright basis on which that reproduction is made and the
attribution string the licence (if any) requires.

---

## Crown copyright (UK judgments)

**Sources used:**
- `caparo_three_stage_test.catala_en` — *Caparo Industries plc v Dickman* [1990] UKHL 2, [1990] 2 AC 605 (passage of Lord Bridge of Harwich).
- `ladd_v_marshall.catala_en` — *Ladd v Marshall* [1954] 1 WLR 1489 (CA) (passage of Denning LJ).
- `english_contract_interpretation.catala_en` — *Wood v Capita Insurance Services Ltd* [2017] UKSC 24 (paragraphs 10–14 of the judgment of Lord Hodge JSC).

**Basis:** UK judgments are Crown copyright, licensed under the
**Open Government Licence v3.0** (`http://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/`),
which permits free reuse with attribution.

**Attribution string (per OGL v3.0):** "Contains public sector
information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0."

## Singapore judgments (elitigation.sg)

**Sources used:**
- `sg_iaa_s_31.catala_en` — *DKT v DKU* [2025] SGCA 23 (paragraph 8, four-condition framework for infra petita challenges).

**Basis:** Singapore judgments are © Government of Singapore. The
elitigation.sg Terms of Use prohibit "systematic reproduction or
storage" of judgments, but allow fair-dealing quotation for purposes
of criticism, review, or research per the Copyright Act 2021 (Cap 63),
in particular s 191 (fair use of judicial decisions).

**Posture:** The reproduced text is approximately 100 words from a
single paragraph of the judgment. This is within fair-dealing for
criticism / review of an academic working paper.

**Attribution string:** "*DKT v DKU* [2025] SGCA 23, ¶ 8, ©
Government of Singapore. Quoted under fair-dealing for criticism /
review per the Copyright Act 2021 (Cap 63), s 191."

## UAE federal law

**Sources used:**
- `uae_civil_code_art_390.catala_en` — UAE Civil Transactions Law (Federal Law No. 5 of 1985), Article 390(1) and 390(2).

**Basis:** UAE federal law is in force across the Emirates;
reproduction of statute text for academic / research purposes is
consistent with standard practice for a primary-law jurisdiction.

**Translation provenance:** The English text quoted is **the
working translation in widest circulation in UAE / DIFC / ADGM
litigation practice**, drawn from the standard practitioner editions.
Where multiple competing translations exist, this module uses the
form most commonly relied upon in ADGM CFI judgments. Where a quoted
phrase materially differs across translations, the encoding's
predicate logic does not depend on the precise English wording —
the Arabic-language original at the UAE Ministry of Justice is the
authoritative source for the rule of decision.

**Attribution string:** "UAE Civil Transactions Law (Federal Law No.
5 of 1985), Article 390. Arabic-language original ©
UAE Ministry of Justice; English working translation from
practitioner editions in common ADGM CFI use."

## Aggregate posture

The verbatim text reproduced under this rule library:

- For UK judgments and Singapore statutes: reproduced under explicit
  open licences (OGL v3.0; SSO Reproduction Notice).
- For DIFC / ADGM / UAE primary law and DIFC Courts rules: reproduced
  for academic / research purposes under the standard fair-dealing
  carve-out, with the rule-of-decision logic in each module being
  the substantive contribution and the verbatim text serving as the
  literate-programming prose against which the encoding is checked
  (Merigoux methodology — see `book.catala-lang.org/en/3-5-lawyers-agile.html`).
- For *DKT v DKU* (Singapore judgment): reproduced under fair-dealing
  for criticism / review per Copyright Act 2021, s 191.

Any rightsholder objecting to the inclusion of any specific quoted
provision should follow the takedown procedure documented in
`/TAKEDOWN.md` (commitment: 7-calendar-day removal from public
repository on verified request).
